# Executive Briefing: User Guide

Updated October 2026

Executive Briefing turns a legal update into a short email for one client executive, with notes on what to check before you send it. It drafts; you review, edit, and send. Treat every draft as a first draft.

**Use it for** a client alert, ruling, or agency action. (Local notes should work but haven’t been tested yet.) **Don't use it for** legal research, court filings, or anything sent without your review. It works only from the source you give it.

## Ask for a briefing

First, confirm that firm policy and the client's outside counsel guidelines allow AI for this work. Then paste or attach the update and name the reader:

> Brief our CFO on this.

For a better email, describe the client in one line. Without client facts, the email would include suggestion lines such as "This applies to you if…".

> Brief the CFO of an $80 million software company in California on this.

## Read your notes

Below the email, each heading tells you what to do:

| Heading | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Fix before sending** | Resolve it first. When one appears, the email divider reads "fix before sending." |
| **Verify before sending** | Check these claims against the source. The client is most likely to act on them. |
| **Add if you know** | Add the client fact to make the email definite, or delete the item. |
| **Not in the source** | Gaps the email relies on. Fill them in or leave them. |
| **Sources** | Each email sentence matched to the source text it came from. |

## Before you send

1. Resolve **Fix before sending** and check each **Verify before sending** claim.
2. Read the email against the listed sources.
3. Add your greeting and sign-off.

To change the draft, reply in plain words, such as "shorter" or "the client is under $1 billion". Only the affected lines change.

## If something looks off

| You see | What to do |
|---|---|
| It asks for the source | Paste the text. A link may not have opened. |
| "There's nothing to brief" | The source isn't a legal update, such as a press release. |
| A **Fix** item about text addressed to an AI | The source contains hidden instructions. The email ignores them; check the source before relying on it. |
