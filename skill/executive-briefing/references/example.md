# Example: A Fictional Matter Update, Briefed for a CFO

This example is entirely fictional. The companies, case, and facts are invented to show the format.

## Source

> FICTIONAL EXAMPLE. Not a real matter.
>
> Matter update, March 3, 2026. Harlow Logistics, Inc. v. Brightline Freight LLC (breach of contract).
>
> On February 27, the court granted in part and denied in part Brightline's motion to dismiss. The court dismissed Harlow's fraud claim but allowed the breach of contract claim to proceed. Harlow seeks $4.2 million in damages for missed delivery commitments under the 2024 master services agreement.
>
> The court ordered the parties to complete mediation by May 15. Document discovery opens March 17.
>
> Brightline's counsel has indicated informally that Brightline may make a settlement offer before mediation. No amount has been discussed.
>
> Harlow should preserve all shipping records and internal emails about the Brightline contract. A litigation hold notice is being prepared and will be circulated separately.

## Request

"Brief our CFO on this."

## Output

**Draft for attorney review: CFO version**

──────── Email (ready to copy) ────────

**Subject:** Action required: Brightline case goes forward; mediation by May 15

On February 27, the court dismissed your fraud claim against Brightline but let the breach of contract claim proceed. Mediation must be completed by **May 15**, and document discovery opens March 17.

This applies to you. Your company still seeks **$4.2 million** in damages, and Brightline may make a settlement offer before mediation; no amount has been discussed.

**Next steps**
1. Preserve all shipping records and internal emails about the Brightline contract.
2. Watch for the litigation hold notice, which will be circulated separately.
3. Decide how you'd respond if Brightline makes an offer before mediation.

As of March 3, 2026.

──────── For the attorney ────────

**Add if you know**
- If you know whether dismissing the fraud claim changes the $4.2 million figure, add it to update the damages amount.

**Sources**
| Email sentence | Source |
|---|---|
| Subject | "allowed the breach of contract claim to proceed"; "complete mediation by May 15" |
| "On February 27, the court…" | "The court dismissed Harlow's fraud claim but allowed the breach of contract claim to proceed." |
| "Mediation must be completed by…" | "complete mediation by May 15"; "Document discovery opens March 17." |
| "This applies to you." | (inferred: the matter is the client's own case) |
| "Your company still seeks $4.2…" | "Harlow seeks $4.2 million in damages"; "may make a settlement offer before mediation"; "No amount has been discussed." |
| "Preserve all shipping records and…" | "preserve all shipping records and internal emails about the Brightline contract" |
| "Watch for the litigation hold…" | "will be circulated separately" |
| "Decide how you'd respond if…" | "may make a settlement offer before mediation" (inferred: an offer would need a response) |
| "As of March 3, 2026." | Matter update dated "March 3, 2026" (private source, so it isn't named) |

## Why the first email works

- It reads like a real attorney update: what happened, what it means for you, next steps. Each part says something the others don't.
- "What happened" is plain: the ruling and the two dates, no legal mechanics.
- "This applies to you." tells the CFO at once that action is needed; the next sentence gives the stake that matters to a CFO (money and a possible offer).
- "Next steps" is numbered and ordered by urgency.
- The source is private, so the email ends "As of [date]" and names no source.
- The attorney section has one item, because only one thing would change the email. Nothing needs verifying: every claim is a direct statement from the notes.
- Hedges survive: the offer stays a "may."

## Second example: the most common case

Most updates are clean: nothing to fix, nothing contestable, no client fact that would change the email. Then the attorney section is just the source table.

### Source

> FICTIONAL EXAMPLE. Not a real matter.
>
> Notes, April 2, 2026. Harlow v. Brightline: the court granted our unopposed motion to extend the deadline to complete document production from April 10 to May 8. No other dates changed.

### Request

"Brief the GC on this."

### Output

**Draft for attorney review: GC version**

──────── Email (ready to copy) ────────

**Subject:** FYI: Brightline document production deadline moved to May 8

The court granted the unopposed motion to extend the deadline to complete document production in your case against Brightline. The new deadline is **May 8**, instead of April 10.

This applies to you. No other dates in the case changed.

**Next steps**
Nothing for now, because the change only adds time.

As of April 2, 2026.

──────── For the attorney ────────

**Sources**
| Email sentence | Source |
|---|---|
| Subject | "extend the deadline to complete document production from April 10 to May 8" |
| "The court granted the unopposed…" | "the court granted our unopposed motion to extend the deadline to complete document production" |
| "The new deadline is May 8,…" | "extend the deadline to complete document production from April 10 to May 8" |
| "This applies to you." | (inferred: the matter is the client's own case) |
| "No other dates in the case…" | "No other dates changed." |
| "Nothing for now, because the…" | "from April 10 to May 8" (inferred: a later deadline requires no action) |
| "As of April 2, 2026." | Notes dated "April 2, 2026" (private source, so it isn't named) |

**Why the attorney section is empty:** every claim is a direct statement from the notes, the conclusion is low-risk (more time, not less), and no client fact would change anything. Each possible item fails the gate: "Without this, the attorney might send something wrong, or miss a decision, because…" has no honest ending.
