# Writing the Email

Read this before drafting. These rules make the email fast to scan and safe to paste into Outlook.

## Writing for scanning

Executives scan: they read first lines, labels, and the first words of each item, and skip items that start the same way.

- **Front-load** each item with what matters: the date, number, or actor ("60 days to file the notice…").
- **Vary how items start.** Don't open several items with the same word.
- **Short sentences,** about 20 words. Split rather than combine; a sentence limit is never a reason to write one long sentence.
- **One idea per item.** If an item needs a number and a long condition, put the number first and the condition in a second sentence.
- **Each fact appears once.** The subject and opening summarize; each part adds something new.
- **Start actions with a verb.**
- **Use plain words** the reader has already seen. If coverage depends on a definition, point to it ("if your firm is covered (see below)").
- **Bold only deadlines and dollar amounts,** at most three in the email.
- **Use numerals** for dates, amounts, and counts.

## The deletion pass

Run it on every email, and on the attorney section, right after writing them. Test each sentence or item against its gate (principle 6 in SKILL.md) and delete what fails. Look in this order, since this is where unearned text usually hides:

1. Anything said twice, in different words or in different parts.
2. Background and history the reader doesn't need to act.
3. Next steps that could be combined (same person, same time).
4. Reasons attached to steps that the step already makes obvious.

Merge items about the same issue into one. Never delete protected content (principle 6).

**Checkpoints, not caps.** If the email is over about 250 words, or the attorney section has more than 3 items, go through once more and test each sentence or item strictly. Keep everything that still passes; a long result then means the source really needs it.

## Formatting

The email must paste cleanly into an email client: bold labels, simple bullets, and numbered lists only. No tables, headers, or special layouts inside the email. (The attorney section may use a table for sources.)

## Reader context block

Attorneys may describe the reader in one line ("CFO of a regional bank") or in a block like this. Use whatever is given; never ask for every field.

```
Reader: [role]
Organization: [type of business]
Size: [revenue, employees, assets, or other measures the law might use]
Locations: [states or countries where they operate]
Relevant activities: [e.g., sells customer data, contracts with the federal government]
Already knows: [prior advice or briefings on this topic]
Preferences: [e.g., wants dollar figures first; prefers very short updates]
```
